We bought pullets once. That's why the passport exists.

When we stocked our own layer house we bought 700 point-of-cage pullets at ₦8,000 each. 41 of them turned out to be cockerels and 92 died before they started laying.

The seller couldn't tell us what the birds weighed, which vaccines they'd had, or how many their batch had lost already. We had to trust them, and it cost us.

So we started rearing our own, in cages, and we write everything down. When you buy from us you see what we see.

That's 16.2% more than the price we were quoted, and it's only the purchase price at point of cage. It leaves out the feed, drugs and labour it took to bring the survivors into lay, so the true cost was higher again. The cheapest pullet is the one that lives and lays.

Why we rear in cages

Cage rearing is what lets us promise even birds with strong immunity. Here's why.

Even birds

Every bird has its own place at the feed trough and the drinker, so the big ones can't push the small ones away. That's where our uniformity comes from.

Off the droppings

The birds never stand in their own litter. Coccidiosis and worms stay low, so each vaccine goes into a healthy bird and actually takes.

Ready for your cages

They've never lived on the floor, so they settle straight into your layer cages without the stress of moving off litter.

What the passport answers

These are the questions we wish we'd been able to ask. The answers come straight out of the farm's daily log, so nobody writes them up afterwards.

Are they the right size for their age?

Mean body weight against the Isa Brown standard for that week, and the date they were last weighed.

Are they even, or a few big birds and many runts?

Uniformity: the share of birds within 10% of the average. We weigh birds one at a time to get it.

How many has this batch lost?

Livability from day-old to the day they leave. We publish it whether it's good or bad.

What have they been vaccinated against?

Every vaccine and treatment, with the day it was given. Anything still due is listed with its date so you can carry on.

Were they brooded properly?

The share of days the brooder stayed in the right temperature range for the chicks' age.

What were they fed?

Each feed and the days it was fed, from starter mash to grower.
